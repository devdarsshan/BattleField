## 1. StudyIQ IAS: "Ahom Military Commander Lachit Borphukan" (3.61M, 15:37)
- **Hook (0:00-0:15):** Hindi, presenter Aadesh Singh. A map turns the Mughal Empire red across India with Assam left out, and he asks how the Mughals took almost all of India but never Assam. A question hook built on the map.
- **Structure:** 0:00 hook and Ahom origins / 1:00 why Ahoms and Mughals clashed / 2:00 Samdhara 1616 / 3:00 1636-39 war and Asurar Ali treaty / 5:00 Mir Jumla 1662, Garhgaon falls / 7:00 Ghilajharighat (harem, indemnity) / 8:00 Ram Singh and Saraighat (says "1671 Aurangzeb sent", wrong) / 9:00 army "75-80,000", Lachit picks Guwahati, river under 1 km / 10:00 fake negotiations to buy time / 11:00 three-sided trap, Munawwar Khan killed / 12:00 1679 Laluk sells Guwahati / 13:00 1682 Itakhuli / 15:00 Lachit Divas, NDA medal.
- **Conflict / comeback:** a flat timeline. The fall of 1662 is the low point and 1671 the comeback, but there is no Alaboi, no illness and no uncle.
- **Payoff:** "Assam was never conquered", with the medal and the day of remembrance.
- **Visuals:** stock maps, stock mountain footage, Hindi subtitles, voice-over. Cheap.
- **Comments:** (1) "Why isn't this in NCERT/school books?" is the biggest theme by far, thousands of likes. (2) "Make a movie / web series like *300*" (Bollywood vs Akbar films). (3) The proverb "Dexot koi momai dangor nohoy", posted by Assamese viewers (3.1K likes), though the video never tells the story. (4) Pronunciation fixes ("It's Gargaon, not Garhganv", 302 likes) and dates ("Ahom rule was 1228-1826"). (5) Outliers: Laluk named as a traitor beside Jaichand; requests for an "Ahom regiment"; viewers say Ahoms kept Buranjis (chronicles). Political comments (illegal migration, "one party") are frequent; avoid feeding them.

## 2. Mighty Monk: "How Ahom Defeated The Mighty Mughals 17 Times?" (505K, 25:15)
- **Hook (0:00-0:30):** Hindi. "You've heard countless Mughal victories; do you know the kingdom that beat them 17 times, with a smaller army, no artillery, no Delhi wealth?" Then guerrilla raids "like ghosts", rumours of demons, a commander who executed his own kin, and enemies sacrificed to gods. A bold claim plus dark teasers.
- **Chapters:** 0:00 intro / 1:35 Ahom dynasty (Sukaphaa 1228, settled peacefully) / 4:38 Mughal ambition, "gates of Assam" (1615-16, 1630s, Mir Jumla) / 12:12 rise of Lachit (his posts, Itakhuli 1667) / 19:46 Saraighat.
- **Conflict / comeback:** a fake one-lakh letter makes the king doubt Lachit (~18:00); the Mughal fleet pushes the Ahoms back while Lachit is sick (~19:30); Lachit rises with "Shall I rest with my wife and children?" and six boats (~20:30); hookah shot; 4,000 dead; Ram Singh's long praise quote (~22:30).
- **Errors:** says Ram Singh also had custody of Guru Tegh Bahadur, who "escaped" (in fact the Guru travelled *with* Ram Singh to Assam). Moves the uncle beheading into the battle speech. Uses the "17 times" claim without support.
- **Payoff:** Lachit dies April 1672; 1682 Itakhuli; 125 ft statue 2024; police academy; NDA medal.
- **Visuals:** AI painterly stills (Aurangzeb on horseback, tents, melee) with slow push-ins and burned-in Hindi subtitles. Cinematic but generic.
- **Comments (1,236):** "We want a movie about the Ahom dynasty" (261); the proverb again (155 and 53); NDA medal "since 1999"; Assamese pride and Ahom identity ("Tai Ahom", "our blood"); people from other states sending love; "Northeast people are not Chinese" (identity); a request to cover Atan Burhagohain's poison arrows ("Singimuri") and dagga juddha; "Hindu adoption" debates.

## 3. Akshaya Kapoor: "The Battle of Saraighat and the Legend of Lachit Borphukan!" (180K, 15:07)
- **Hook (0:00-0:15):** "The Ukraine-Russia war reminds me of a great Indian battle", a big empire against a small neighbour. A news hook from April 2022 that has dated. Then army numbers on screen and "like the movie *300*, except the small side won".
- **Structure:** 0:00 hook / 1:26 names the battle / 2:19 Mir Jumla (says "1680", wrong; 1662) / 3:46 treaty terms in kg / 4:28 king dies of shame, brother swears revenge / 5:40 Itakhuli 1667 / 6:10 invented court scene ("one man... Hengdang") / 6:35 Ram Singh's army / 8:07 Lachit's plan (walls, narrows, Andharubali) / 9:53 duel challenge refused / 10:47 new king's council; only Atan says no / 11:33 Munawwar Khan sent, "sent to fight, not to make treaties" / 12:08 Lachit sick, Nara in command / 12:25 Lachit rises, 7 boats / 13:12 hookah shot, 4,000 dead / 13:57 Lachit Divas, NDA medal, "better than Leonidas".
- **Conflict / comeback:** a clean underdog arc with the sick commander's comeback at about 80%. **Leaves out Alaboi**, the uncle, the arrow letter, Lachit's death and the 1679 betrayal.
- **Visuals:** talking head on green screen (war painting behind), 2D map with arrows and icon-based army charts. English captions on a Hindi voice track.
- **Comments (851):** "A movie like *300*" (96); the uncle story and proverb (256, the top substantive comment); "Atan Burhagohain needs more recognition"; pronunciation fixes ("Borphukan, not Barfuka"); "there were many Hindu kings with the Mughals against the Ahoms; Ram Singh was one" (an outlier, and accurate); a claim that the hengdang was 35 kg and 5 ft (myth); "Haldighati was taught but not Saraighat"; "this is only 50% of the story".

(Also noted: the DIIISHOOM 2:23 "cinematic trailer" (79K) proves the river battle sells as spectacle. The 2.8M Short is a Hindi hype clip.)

## What we take from this
- **Our edge is English, animation and the river.** Every top video is Hindi, a lecture or AI stills. A 3D diorama of Guwahati's hills pinching the Brahmaputra, with the boat-bridge closing on the Mughal fleet, is something none of them have. It also gives the US audience a geography they can follow.
- **Tell the defeat first.** None of the big three dramatise Alaboi (1669: the king overrides Lachit, the cavalry cuts them down, by one account 10,000 dead). It is the "all is lost" beat that makes March 1671 land, and it explains why Lachit later refuses to chase.
- **Answer the top comment outliers on screen:** (1) the uncle proverb, told and honestly labelled as tradition; (2) "the commander on the Mughal side was a Hindu Rajput", a fact that defuses the communal framing and makes the story richer (Ram Singh, the Shivaji escape that sent him east, Guru Tegh Bahadur riding with him); (3) Atan Burhagohain's credit (the council "no", clearing Lachit of the bribe letter); (4) pronunciation: say "Lachit Bor-PHU-kon" and "Gargaon" right, maybe with a pinned note; (5) correct the "17 times" myth without a lecture.
- **Use the betrayal epilogue.** 1679: Lachit's own brother hands Guwahati over in a secret deal; 1682: it is taken back for good. Only StudyIQ mentions it, and only in passing. It is a strong final turn after the obvious ending.
- **Give the enemy a voice.** Ram Singh's quoted praise ("one single individual leads all the forces") is the strongest closing line in the material. Attribute it to the Assamese chronicle.
- **Don't date the hook.** Avoid news pegs (Ukraine aged Kapoor's video). The *300* comparison is what viewers reach for themselves; we can reference it once and flip it ("the small side didn't die, it won").
- **Hook candidates (no spoilers):**
  1. *Question:* "In 1671 the richest empire on earth sent an army up the Brahmaputra to finish a kingdom it had already beaten once. The man standing in its way couldn't even stand up. What could he possibly do?"
  2. *Bold claim:* "The Mughals took Delhi, Bengal and the Deccan. They took the Ahom capital too. Then the whole war came down to one stretch of river barely a kilometre wide."
  3. *Image:* a fevered general being carried down to a small boat at night while dozens of Ahom boats turn back upstream. "His own fleet was running. He had six boats."
