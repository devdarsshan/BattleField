# Saraighat: Sources and fact notes

Three layers of evidence, kept apart throughout:
- **Chronicle**: the Ahom Buranjis (court chronicles in Assamese and Tai-Ahom, some written by participants or near-contemporaries) and the Persian side (Alamgirnamah, Maasir-i-Alamgiri, Fathiya-i-Ibriya for Mir Jumla). The best-known Saraighat account is *Ram Singhar Yuddhar Katha*, reproduced by S. K. Bhuyan.
- **Scholarship**: Gait (1906), S. K. Bhuyan (1947), J. N. Sarkar (1992), Jadunath Sarkar (1994), S. L. Baruah (1986).
- **Tradition**: proverbs, folk songs, later retellings. Tagged [TRAD] below.

## Fact sheet

### Background: the humiliation (1662-1663)
1. Ahom kingdom: ruled the Brahmaputra valley from the 13th century (founder Sukaphaa, 1228) until British annexation in 1826. [WP-SG][GAIT]
2. Earlier fighting with the Mughals from 1615; a treaty of 1639 (Asurar Ali) set the boundary at the Barnadi / Asurar Ali, leaving Guwahati with the Mughals. [GAIT][WP-SG]
3. During Shah Jahan's succession war (1658) the Ahom king Jayadhwaj Singha pushed west and took Guwahati. Aurangzeb sent Mir Jumla, governor of Bengal, who invaded in early 1662. [GAIT][WP-SG]
4. Mir Jumla took the fort of Simalugarh and then the Ahom capital Garhgaon (March 1662); the king fled to the hills of Namrup. The monsoon then cut the Mughal army off; disease and night raids wore it down. [GAIT][WP-SG]
5. Treaty of Ghilajharighat, 23 Jan 1663 (Sarkar; one Persian-based account gives late Dec 1662). Terms per Sarkar via [WP-TG]:
   - the king's daughter (Ramani Gabharu) sent to the imperial harem, with the daughter of the Tipam raja;
   - 20,000 tolas gold, 120,000 tolas silver, 20 elephants at once; 300,000 tolas silver and 90 elephants within a year; 20 elephants a year after;
   - four hostages: sons of the Burhagohain, Borgohain, Borpatrogohain and Garhgaya Phukan;
   - all land west of the Bharali (north bank) and Kalang (south bank) ceded, which put Guwahati in Mughal hands.
   The terms were only partly fulfilled. [WP-TG]
6. Mir Jumla died on the way back to Bengal (1663). Jayadhwaj Singha died soon after the treaty (1663). Tradition says he died of shame and charged his successor to remove "the spear of disgrace". The deathbed charge is [TRAD]. [GAIT for deaths]
7. Chakradhwaj Singha (r. 1663-1669) refused to keep paying, rebuilt forts and the army, and decided to fight when the Mughal faujdar Firuz Khan pressed for the arrears (1667). His nobles warned against it; he overrode them. [GAIT]

### Lachit and the retaking of Guwahati (1667)
8. Lachit was the son of Momai Tamuli Borbarua, a commoner who rose to Borbarua under Pratap Singha. Traditional birth date 24 Nov 1622 (Lachit Divas is held on this day); the exact date is not in the contemporary record. [WP-LB][GAIT "the son of the Bar Barua"]
9. Before command he held posts such as Ghora Barua (royal stables), commander at Simalugarh, and Dolakasharia Barua (household guard). [popular sources, consistent; WP-LB]
10. August 1667: an army set out under Lachit, newly made Borphukan (viceroy and commander of the west). Popular sources say the king gave him a gold-hafted sword, the **hengdang**, as the mark of office. Bhuyan-based retellings carry this; the hengdang is a standard Ahom insignia. Claims that it weighed 35 kg are [TRAD] and should be dropped. [GAIT for the date; hengdang via popular sources]
11. The Ahoms took Kajali and Bansbari, then besieged Guwahati and Pandu. The fort of Itakhuli was stormed at night (WP gives 4 Nov 1667) after about two months. A Mughal relief force was beaten back to the Manas; Firuz Khan was captured. [GAIT][WP-SG]
12. Tradition: Bagh Hazarika crept in and wetted the Mughal cannon and powder before the assault. [WP-SG note; treat as tradition]
13. Inscriptions on captured cannon name Chakradhwaj Singha as "slayer of his enemies" (1667-68). A rock inscription near Kanai Barasi records an Ahom fort built in 1667. [GAIT]

### The Mughal side
14. Aurangzeb heard of the loss in December 1667 and appointed Raja Ram Singh I of Amber (Kachhwaha Rajput, son of Mirza Raja Jai Singh). Ram Singh left Delhi on 27 Dec 1667 and reached Rangamati (frontier post) in February 1669. [GAIT][WP-SG]
15. Ram Singh and Shivaji: in 1666 Ram Singh had custody of Shivaji in Agra; Shivaji escaped (17 Aug 1666). Ram Singh was suspected, barred from court and lowered in rank. Several sources say the Assam command was a punishment posting; "service in Assam was very unpopular". The link is a historian's inference, not a stated order. [WP-RS, citing Jadunath Sarkar]
16. Army size, every figure found:
   - Gait, from the Alamgirnamah: 18,000 cavalry, 30,000 infantry, 15,000 Koch archers. (The archive.org OCR reads "80,000"; Sarkar gives 30,000.) [GAIT]
   - J. N. Sarkar 1992: Ram Singh's own 4,000 troopers, 1,500 ahadis, 500 barqandazes, plus 30,000 infantry, 21 Rajput chiefs with contingents, 18,000 cavalry, 2,000 archers and shieldmen, 40 ships. [WP-SG note 4]
   - Same article body: 10,000 Koch archers and shieldmen (contradicts its own footnote). [WP-SG]
   - Ram Singh I page: 4,000 Rajputs, 1,500 ahadis, 500 artillerymen, 15,000 Koch archers; one footnote says 8,000 troopers. [WP-RS]
   - Popular videos: "75,000-80,000" (StudyIQ), "10 times the Ahom army" (Akshaya Kapoor). No primary source for either.
   - Ahom side: Sarkar (Jaipur, p. 147): "being a nation in arms, mustered 100,000". 20,000 more sent under Atan Burhagohain in 1670-71 (Baruah p. 275). [WP-SG][WP-RS]
17. Ram Singh brought Guru Tegh Bahadur, the ninth Sikh Guru, with him from Dhaka. The Guru camped at Dhubri while Ram Singh camped at Rangamati (early 1669). Sikh tradition says he urged Ram Singh to negotiate; the Dhubri gurdwara marks the spot. [SIKH; tradition-based, no Ahom or Persian confirmation]

### The long campaign (1669-1671)
18. Lachit chose Guwahati: hills on both banks (Kamakhya/Nilachal, Itakhuli, Sukreswar on the south; Aswakranta on the north), with gaps closed by earthen ramparts, so cavalry could not get round and the Mughals had to fight on the river. HQ at Andharubali. [WP-SG][GAIT]
19. The Brahmaputra at Saraighat is about 1 km wide at its narrowest. [WP-SG]
20. Ahom tactics: night raids and guerrilla war (dagga juddha), led partly by Atan Burhagohain; small fast boats against heavy Mughal boats (some carried up to 16 guns). [WP-SG]
21. Opening exchange: the Ahoms asked why he was invading; Ram Singh demanded the old 1639 boundary. The Borphukan replied he would rather fight than give up an inch. [GAIT]
22. 1669 fighting near Tezpur and Sualkuchi went both ways. Ram Singh quarrelled with Rashid Khan, suspected him of siding with the Ahoms and cut his tent ropes. [GAIT]
23. Ram Singh challenged Chakradhwaj Singha to single combat ("if beaten I go back to Bengal"). The king refused, calling a duel with a servant of the emperor beneath him. Gait reports it with "it is said". [GAIT][WP-AL]
24. **Battle of Alaboi** (c. 5 Aug 1669): the king ordered an open-field attack against Lachit's advice. Rajput cavalry routed the Ahoms. Baruah's figure: 10,000 Ahom dead. Gait: Lachit "was severely censured by the king". The Buranjis name a woman warrior, Madanavati, in the Mughal van; Persian sources do not mention her. [WP-AL][GAIT]
25. Aurangzeb raised Ram Singh's rank from 4,000 to 5,000 after Alaboi. [WP-AL]
26. Ram Singh complained that night attacks were beneath "the dignity of warfare" and paused operations (Oct 1669 - Mar 1670). Popular retelling gives Lachit's reply as "lions fight at night"; no chronicle source found. [WP-SG][LHI; reply = TRAD]
27. **Arrow letter / one lakh**: Ram Singh had a letter shot into the Ahom camp (some versions: sent to the court) addressed to Lachit, saying he had taken 100,000 rupees to abandon Guwahati. Chakradhwaj Singha grew suspicious; Atan Burhagohain persuaded him it was a trick. Found only in popular retellings that rely on Bhuyan; not in Gait or Wikipedia. [SIQ][Mighty Monk video]
28. Ram Singh offered to withdraw for a return to the 1639 boundary plus 300,000 rupees; also tried bribing individual Phukans. [WP-SG]
29. Chakradhwaj Singha died (1669/70). His brother Udayaditya Singha succeeded. [GAIT]
30. In council, most commanders leaned toward Ram Singh's terms; Atan Burhagohain alone argued Delhi would not keep them and the war's cost would be wasted. The proposal was rejected. Gait says the Borphukan agreed to the old boundary while waiting for confirmation, and Ram Singh then advanced. [WP-SG][GAIT]
31. Shaista Khan (Bengal) sent reinforcements and a message: Ram Singh was sent to fight the Assamese, not befriend them. Admiral Munawwar Khan brought the fleet. [WP-SG]

### Saraighat (March 1671)
32. Date: the exact day is unknown; mid-March 1671. Gait: by March 1671 Ram Singh "had become so weakened by repeated losses that he retreated". Gait admits the Buranjis are "confused" for this phase. [WP-SG][GAIT]
33. A Mughal envoy reported a breach in the Andharubali rampart. Ram Singh moved along the north bank; five Mughal captains (two firingis, i.e. Europeans) joined near Sualkuchi with artillery. [WP-SG]
34. Lachit and the Ahom admiral were both seriously ill; Nara Hazarika (some say Nara Raja) held command. [WP-SG][Akshaya Kapoor video]
35. Near Aswakranta, Laluksola Phukan's land force beat the Mughals, but Mughal boats drove the Ahom fleet back to Barhila; the land force withdrew for fear of being cut off. Ahom boats began slipping upstream. [WP-SG][GAIT "navy was forced to retreat to Barhila"]
36. Lachit, watching from his sickbed at Itakhuli, ordered every unit to attack and took to the river with a small flotilla: six boats (WP, Swarajya) or seven (Akshaya Kapoor, Organiser).
37. His words, as given via the Buranji: "The King has put all the people in my hands to fight the Bongal. Shall I go back to my wife and children?" ("Bongal" = outsider/Mughal). Longer "speeches" in videos and articles are later paraphrase. [WP-SG][TRAD for longer versions]
38. Ahom boats converged at Amrajuli, lashed together as a boat-bridge, and struck from front and rear. [WP-SG]
39. Munawwar Khan was shot (from behind, reportedly while smoking a hookah). The Mughal fleet broke. Mughal losses: three senior amirs and about 4,000 men. No Ahom casualty figure exists. [WP-SG]
40. The Mughals fell back to the Manas. Lachit forbade a deep pursuit, remembering Alaboi. Ram Singh left Kamrup on 7 April 1671 for Rangamati. [WP-SG]
41. Ram Singh's praise (via Bhuyan's translation of the Assamese account; wording as widely reproduced): "Glory to the king! Glory to the counsellors! Glory to the commanders! Glory to the country! One single individual leads all the forces! Even I, Ram Singha, being personally present on the spot, have not been able to find any loophole and opportunity!" A second passage (Raj Bhavan booklet, Mighty Monk) has him say every Assamese soldier can row, shoot arrows, dig trenches and fire guns and cannon, and that he had seen no such all-round army anywhere in India. Source chain: Assamese chronicle tradition, not a Persian record. [ORG][Raj Bhavan][TRAD-chronicle]
42. Ram Singh stayed based at Rangamati and was received by Aurangzeb only on 25 June 1676; the Ram Singh I page says he was demoted. He died in Kohat on 10 April 1688. [WP-SG][WP-RS]
43. Udayaditya Singha loaded the Borphukan with presents; the frontier moved to Hadira opposite Goalpara. [GAIT]

### Lachit's uncle (tradition)
44. While the ramparts were being built (traditionally at Amingaon / on the north bank), Lachit found his maternal uncle neglecting the work and beheaded him, saying in Assamese **"Dexot koi momai dangor nohoy"** ("my uncle is not greater than my country"). It is a well-known proverb in Assam. The uncle is not named in our sources, and the Sentinel calls the story "believed, however not verified". Not in Gait or the Wikipedia articles. [TRAD][SIQ][Sentinel]

### Aftermath and memory
45. Lachit died about a year after the battle: 25 April 1672 at Kaliabor per Wikipedia (one popular source says Jorhat; most say April 1672). Cause: illness. Wikipedia notes it was not from the Saraighat illness; no evidence of poisoning. [WP-LB][WP-SG]
46. Buried in a maidam at Hoolungapara (Hollongapar), near Teok, Jorhat, built by Udayaditya Singha. [WP-LB][NEWS]
47. 1679: Laluksola Borphukan, Lachit's brother and successor at Guwahati, handed Guwahati to the Mughals in a deal with Prince Muhammad Azam (governor of Bengal), hoping to be made king. Some sources give a bribe of 400,000 rupees (StudyIQ); not in Gait. Gait: surrendered "early in March 1679". [GAIT][WP-SG]
48. 1682: under Gadadhar Singha the Ahoms retook Guwahati at the Battle of Itakhuli; Gait calls it "the last Muhammadan war". The Manas then became the accepted boundary. Ahom rule lasted until 1826. [GAIT][WP-SG]
49. "The Ahoms beat the Mughals 17 times": a popular claim. Some writers use 17 for the number of Mughal attacks from 1615 to 1682, not Ahom wins. No historian's count found. The record was mixed (1662-63 was a clear Mughal win). [search results, see flagged table]
50. Lachit Divas, 24 November, observed in Assam since the early 20th century. [WP-LB]
51. NDA: the Lachit Borphukan Gold Medal for the best passing-out cadet, instituted in 1999 under Army chief Gen. V. P. Malik. [LHI; Wikipedia gives no year]
52. 400th birth anniversary: year-long celebration ending in New Delhi, 23-25 Nov 2022, with the PM at Vigyan Bhawan on 25 Nov. [NEWS]
53. 9 March 2024: PM Modi unveiled a 125 ft bronze statue (84 ft figure on a 41 ft pedestal) by Ram Sutar at the Lachit Barphukan Maidam project, Hollongapar, Jorhat; foundation laid Feb 2022 by President Kovind. Commonly called the "Statue of Valour". [NEWS]
54. 16 July 2026: Assam CM Himanta Biswa Sarma said the state is in talks with Aditya Dhar to direct a Hindi biopic. Talks only; no deal announced as of this file. [NEWS]
55. A 35 ft statue of Lachit stands in the Brahmaputra off Guwahati (2016). [LHI]
56. Lachit's memory is politically contested: historians in Assam have objected to recent framings of him as a Hindu-vs-Muslim figure (Scroll, 2022, cited in WP-LB). The Mughal commander was a Hindu Rajput; Mughal forces included Rajputs and Koch archers, and the Ahom side had Muslim soldiers in its service (popular claim, e.g. Bagh Hazarika as Ismail Siddique; tradition, verify).
