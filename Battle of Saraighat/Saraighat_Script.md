## HOOK

[S01] A commander lies sick with a fever, on a hill above a great river. Below him, his own war boats are turning and rowing away. Behind them comes the war fleet of the Mughal Empire.

[S02] Only a handful of boats are still near him. So, what can one sick commander with a handful of boats do against an empire?

[S03] The Brahmaputra river, at Guwahati, in the far north-east of India. March, sixteen seventy-one. The sick man is Lachit Borphukan, commander of the Ahom kingdom of Assam. The fleet belongs to Aurangzeb, emperor of the Mughals.

[S04] Mughal records list eighteen thousand horsemen in this army alone. Nine years ago, the same empire marched into Assam and took its capital. By every count of men and guns, it should happen again.

[S05] But here at Guwahati, the river squeezes between hills, barely one kilometre wide. The whole war will come down to this one stretch of water. So, how did an empire end up fighting for a kilometre of river? It starts with a defeat.

## SEGMENT 1: THE DEFEAT

[S06] Assam, sixteen sixty-two. Picture a map, with north at the top. A great river runs through a long green valley. For more than four hundred years, this valley has belonged to the Ahoms. Their court keeps chronicles called Buranjis, records of every reign and every war.

[S07] To the west, the Mughal Empire rules most of India from Delhi. On our map, Mughal land is green and Ahom land is red. In sixteen sixty-two, the Mughal governor of Bengal, Mir Jumla, comes up the river with a huge army.

[S08] He takes the Ahom forts one by one, then Garhgaon, the Ahom capital. The king flees into the hills. Rain, disease and night raids wear the invaders down, but the Ahoms are beaten.

[S09] The treaty is harsh. The king's daughter goes to the emperor's harem, the women's palace. Four nobles hand over their sons as hostages. Gold, silver and elephants go west. And Guwahati becomes Mughal land.

## SEGMENT 2: THE NEW COMMANDER

[S10] The next king, Chakradhwaj Singha, stops paying and rebuilds his army. In sixteen sixty-seven, his nobles beg him to keep the peace. He decides to fight. For that, he needs a commander. Meet Lachit.

[S11] His father began life as a commoner and rose to one of the highest posts in the kingdom. Now the king makes Lachit the Borphukan, commander of the whole western frontier. Accounts say the king hands him a gold-handled sword, the hengdang.

[S12] In November sixteen sixty-seven, Lachit's men storm the fort of Itakhuli at night. Guwahati is Ahom again. A month later, the news reaches Aurangzeb. He sends an army to take it back.

## SEGMENT 3: THE PRINCE FROM AMBER

[S13] Meet Raja Ram Singh, prince of Amber, a Rajput kingdom near the modern city of Jaipur. He is a Hindu, and one of the emperor's senior generals. And he is in trouble.

[S14] In sixteen sixty-six, he was in charge of a famous prisoner in Agra: Shivaji, the Maratha king. Shivaji escaped. The emperor suspected Ram Singh and lowered his rank. Many historians think this posting, to the rain and fevers of Assam, is his punishment.

[S15] Travelling with his army is Guru Tegh Bahadur, the ninth Guru of the Sikhs. Sikh tradition says the Guru urged him to make peace. Besides the horsemen, the records list foot soldiers and archers in their tens of thousands, cannon, and a fleet of war boats.

## SEGMENT 4: THE PLAN

[S16] Lachit knows he cannot beat Mughal cavalry on open ground. So he chooses where to fight. Now look at Guwahati. Hills stand on both banks. Between them, his men raise long earthen walls, so horsemen cannot ride around.

[S17] To go further, the Mughals must come up the river by boat, through the narrows at Saraighat. Remember the width: barely one kilometre. The big Mughal boats carry up to sixteen cannon each. Ahom boats are small and fast.

[S18] Assam remembers a story from these days. Lachit finds a stretch of wall unfinished. The officer in charge is his own uncle. Lachit has him beheaded on the spot.

[S19] His words are still a proverb in Assam: "My uncle is not greater than my country." No surviving chronicle confirms the story. Almost everyone in Assam knows it anyway.

## SEGMENT 5: ALABOI

[S20] August, sixteen sixty-nine. A plain called Alaboi, near Guwahati. The king orders an attack in open country. Lachit argues against it. The king overrules him.

[S21] It is the fight Lachit feared most. Rajput cavalry rides down the Ahom foot soldiers. By one account, ten thousand Ahoms die. The king blames Lachit. Remember Alaboi. Lachit never forgets it.

## SEGMENT 6: LETTERS AND TRICKS

[S22] After Alaboi, the Ahoms go back to night attacks. Small parties strike the Mughal camps in the dark and vanish into the hills. So Ram Singh tries another weapon: doubt.

[S23] According to the Assamese accounts, an arrow lands in the Ahom camp with a letter for Lachit. It thanks him for taking one hundred thousand rupees to give up Guwahati.

[S24] There was no such deal. But the king starts to wonder. Meet Atan Burhagohain, the chief minister. He tells the king the letter is a trick, meant to split them apart. The king listens.

[S25] Then the king dies, and his brother takes the throne. Ram Singh offers a deal: go back to the old border, and he will leave. Most of the council wants to accept. Atan Burhagohain says Delhi will not keep its word. The offer is refused.

## SEGMENT 7: THE RIVER

[S26] March, sixteen seventy-one. Ram Singh moves. His army comes along the north bank, and a new fleet comes up the river toward the narrows. And now, at the worst moment, Lachit is sick.

[S27] He lies in his camp at Itakhuli, on the hill above the river, too ill to lead. On the bank, an Ahom land force beats the Mughals back. On the water, it goes the other way.

[S28] The heavy Mughal boats drive the Ahom fleet upstream, and crews start slipping away. From the hill, Lachit watches his own fleet run.

[S29] He has lost once before, when he could not choose the ground. Now the ground is his, and the river is slipping away. If the Mughal boats pass the narrows, the valley lies open.

[S30] He orders every unit to attack. Then he goes down to the river and climbs into a boat. Only six or seven boats go with him, by different accounts.

[S31] The Assamese chronicle gives his words: "The king has put all the people in my hands to fight the Bongal," the outsiders from the west. "Shall I go back to my wife and children?"

## SEGMENT 8: THE BRIDGE OF BOATS

[S32] The Ahom boats turn back and gather around him. They lash their boats together, side by side, into a floating bridge across the river. In the narrows, the Mughal fleet is caught, struck from the front and from behind.

[S33] The Mughal admiral, Munawwar Khan, is on his boat. The Assamese account says he is smoking a hookah, a tall water pipe, when a shot hits him. He falls dead.

[S34] With its admiral gone, the Mughal fleet breaks. By one count, about four thousand Mughal soldiers die. Ahom losses are not recorded.

[S35] Lachit's men want to chase the enemy down the river. He holds them back. You know why. Alaboi. In April, Ram Singh withdraws to the frontier.

## SEGMENT 9: AFTER THE RIVER

[S36] The Assamese chronicle puts praise in Ram Singh's mouth: "Glory to the king! Glory to the counsellors! Glory to the commanders! Glory to the country! One single individual leads all the forces!" No Mughal record repeats it.

[S37] Lachit dies of illness about a year later, in April sixteen seventy-two. And now, a final twist. In sixteen seventy-nine, his own brother, commanding at Guwahati, hands the city to the Mughals in a secret deal, hoping to be made king.

[S38] It does not last. In sixteen eighty-two, the Ahoms take Guwahati back at Itakhuli, the fort Lachit stormed. It is the last Mughal war in Assam. Ahom kings rule here until eighteen twenty-six, when the valley passes to the British.

[S39] Today, the twenty-fourth of November is Lachit Day in Assam, and the best cadet at India's National Defence Academy receives a gold medal in his name. A popular claim says the Ahoms beat the Mughals seventeen times. No historian's count supports that number.

## ENDING

[S40] So, why did the Mughals lose at Saraighat? Lachit chose the ground, and the hills forced the war onto the water. Small, fast boats fought in a river they knew. Atan Burhagohain kept the council from giving in.

[S41] And when the fleet ran, a sick man went down to the river. The Mughal commander was a Hindu prince, and a Sikh Guru travelled with his army. At Saraighat, an empire met a kingdom that refused to be part of it.

[S42] A river one kilometre wide. A fever. A handful of boats. And an empire that went home.
