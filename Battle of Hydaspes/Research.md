## 1. BazBattles: "Alexander the Great: Battle of the Hydaspes 326 BC" (1.65M, 12:46)
- **Hook (0:00-0:35):** after the sponsor line, "May, 326 BC. The unbeaten Macedonian army invades Punjab. In pouring rain the insatiable Alexander stands on the west bank of the Hydaspes... his last great battle against a formidable Indian king is about to happen." Mood and stakes, no question.
- **Structure:** 0:35-6:00 is a **recap** of Persepolis, Darius, Bessus, the Philotas/Parmenion purge and Roxana; almost half the runtime has nothing to do with the battle. 6:00 Taxila and Porus refuses. 7:00 river, monsoon, feints. 7:30 crossing 30 km upstream in a storm, Porus's son (2,000 horse) killed. 8:30 deployment ("more than 100 elephants"). 9:00-10:30 the battle (horse archers, Companions on the left, Coenus round the right, elephants panic). 10:40 casualties (~1,000 Macedonians), "over 7 feet tall", the quote. 11:08 sponsor.
- **Conflict/comeback:** elephants briefly break into the phalanx, then Coenus's hammer blow. Porus "knew he had lost yet refused to surrender".
- **Payoff:** "Treat me like a king would treat another king" (the modern misquote). Porus made satrap. No Hyphasis, no "who won" discussion.
- **Visuals:** 2D top-down campaign map with unit blocks and coin-portrait markers *(seen)*. Commenters praise the maps.
- **Errors/gaps:** says it was the Macedonians' first time facing elephants; says the sarissas were lengthened for the battle (unsourced).
- **Comment themes:** jokes ("GG" from Alexander at 11:12, "I'm tired of winning" at 3:35), respect for Porus (1.8K: "Porus had balls of steel... outnumbered 2:1"), Bucephalus not mentioned (1K likes). Indian-side outliers: the African proverb "Until the lions have their own historians..." (804); the full Plutarch 62 passage about Porus "blunting their courage" pasted in (748); a **fake** letter from Alexander to his mother ("every mother in this land has brought an Alexander into this world", 553 likes; it has no ancient source). One commenter points out the second-island mistake the video left out.

## 2. Kings and Generals: "Battle of Hydaspes 326 BC" (502K, 24:45)
- **Hook (0:00-0:40):** "From Taxila to the Hyphasis would prove the most difficult part of Alexander's career: his greatest extent, one of his toughest yet most brilliant victories, and his men pushed to their breaking point." Then a 1-minute sponsor read.
- **Structure:** 1:46 Nanda empire context. 2:15 envoys to Porus and Abisares; Porus "will meet him at his border". 3:20 the armies (35,000 infantry, 4,000 cavalry, 300 chariots, about 100 elephants: mixed numbers, unsourced). 4:20 feints. 5:20 crossing, the island mistake. 7:20 the son's chariots. 8:45-12:10 the battle in detail. 12:38 "Treat me as a king". 13:00 analysis and casualties (prefers Diodorus). **14:00-16:50 a myth-busting section: "did Porus actually win?"** It covers Justin and the *Alexander Romance*, the restored-king pattern, and Bucephalus, and ends calling the theory "poor history at best, rank propaganda at worst". 16:50 Sangala. 18:50-23:00 the Hyphasis: speeches, Coenus, three days in the tent, the omens, 12 altars, Arrian's "defeated only by his own men". 23:10 Coenus dies.
- **Conflict/comeback:** the surprise is lost when Porus's scouts spot them; Alexander improvises.
- **Payoff:** the Hyphasis. The army, not an enemy, stops him.
- **Visuals:** illustrated 2D animated maps and figures *(K&G house style; unverified for this episode)*.
- **Comment themes:** Bucephalus grief (511, 608), awe at the tactics, respect for Porus (142: "fighting until he almost passed out"). Indian commenters: "our school books say Alexander won but Porus was honourable; I never heard of a defeat in India" (18, 19 replies); recommends the 1991 *Chanakya* TV serial (250); "do Seleucus vs Maurya, Huns vs Guptas". The scriptwriter's pinned Q&A comment has 2.1K likes and 232 replies, so viewers want to argue with sources.

## 3. Keerthi History: "Alexander vs Porus | Truth Uncovered" (1.29M, 7:47)
- **Hook (0:00-0:25):** "We've all been told Alexander defeated Porus, was so impressed he gave the kingdom back, then went home because his soldiers were tired. But this story has a lot of loopholes. Do we have any evidence?" A "you were lied to" frame aimed at the textbook story.
- **Structure:** 0:25-2:45 standard battle summary (Ambhi, flood, the son, mud, 200 elephants, "like a king"). 2:45-5:30 argument from silence: "no Indian record of Alexander or Porus"; Taxila ("10,000 scholars") was nearby; "Indian records widely talk about Seleucus" (false: those records are Greek). 5:30-7:47 three options: (1) no war happened, (2) too small to record, (3) Porus won, because Bucephalus died, a tyrant would not have spared Porus, he turned back, and he "died on the way back, maybe from these injuries". Ends: "this will remain a mystery".
- **Conflict/comeback:** none in the battle itself. The conflict is viewer against textbook.
- **Payoff:** an open question with a lean toward Porus. Several factual errors (Seleucus, the cause of Alexander's death, "Greek records aren't primary").
- **Visuals:** presenter-led explainer with images *(unverified)*. One commenter mentions a sleeping cat in the background, so it is filmed at home.
- **Comment themes:** mostly support ("we were taught wrong history", "rewrite our history", "my teacher told me to just write it in exams" 194). Outliers and claims to answer: "Chinese records say Alexander was defeated" (346); "Chinese and Persian records say Porus spared him and gave him Indian steel swords" (394); the Oliver Stone film shows Porus killing Alexander (1K; wrong, the film shows Alexander wounded); Zhukov; Aitzaz Ahsan's *Indus Saga*; "Alexander killed his own relatives, so why spare Porus?" (655); "locals near Jhelum believe Porus won" (95). Sceptics inside the thread: "if Porus won, why no Indian record either?" (206, 112); "you say Porus may not have existed, then that he killed the horse" (contradiction noted); "Alexander didn't give the kingdom back, he made him a puppet, as he did with every king".

## What we take from this
- **The gap: nobody gives the honest two-part answer.** BazBattles skips the question. K&G dismisses it in three minutes and calls it propaganda, which alienates Indian viewers. Keerthi and StudyIQ leave it as "mystery" or "vote". Nobody says what the sources actually support: *Alexander won the battle; Porus won the peace; the Beas ended the march.* That gives an answer both the US and the Indian audience can accept, and it can be backed line by line.
- **Show the sources as characters.** No competitor explains that the "Greek version" is several later writers who disagree with each other (Ptolemy vs Aristobulus on the son, 85 vs 200 elephants, Arrian's 310 dead vs Diodorus's ~1,000). Showing that they disagree, and that even so none of them records a loss, is the strongest move in the "who won" argument, and it treats the scepticism fairly.
- **Strong scenes they skip or rush:** the grain-hoarding bluff; the nightly war cries; Attalus dressed as Alexander; the storm; the wrong island and chest-deep ford; Porus nearly spearing Taxiles; the elephant pulling spears out of its king; Meroes the old friend; Porus "everything is in that request"; the three days in the tent at the Beas. Each is a good beat for a low-poly diorama.
- **Comment outliers to answer on screen, briefly and without mockery:** (1) "Treat me as a king treats a king" isn't in any source; (2) Alexander was not killed by Porus. His near-fatal wound came a year later, and he died in Babylon in 323; (3) Indian sources don't mention Seleucus either; his treaty is in Greek sources; (4) the "every mother bore an Alexander" letter is fake; (5) the Romance and *Shahnameh*, the "other sources", have Alexander win; (6) "Purushottam" is modern; Paurava is the likely root. And give the Indian side its real strongest card: Plutarch himself says Porus "blunted their courage".
- **Elephants are the emotional engine for the Indian audience** (the 2.7K top comment). Make them heroes and victims. Arrian says the stampede killed more Indians than Greeks, which is a tragic beat.
- **Packaging lesson:** the 8.25M Short works because it challenges the textbook story in the first sentence and lets the comments fight. Our long-form should open with the same challenge ("you were told...") but promise evidence, not a "mystery". Cut a 45-second Short on "the one sentence Plutarch wrote that Indian YouTube loves", which drives viewers to the full answer.
- **Keep the poll idea, but after the answer.** End with "now you've seen the evidence: battle, peace, or march — what would you call it?" to keep the comment debate StudyIQ gets.
