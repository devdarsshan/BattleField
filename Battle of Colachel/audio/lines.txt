S01|A fishing village called Colachel, at the southern tip of India. August, seventeen forty-one.
S02|On the beach stands a wall of sharpened wooden posts. Behind it wait the soldiers of the Dutch East India Company, a trading company from the Netherlands with its own ships, its own forts, and its own army.
S03|Outside the wall is the army of Travancore, a small kingdom on the south-west coast of India. Its king is Marthanda Varma. He is thirty-six years old.
S04|The Dutch came here to punish him. They have ships, cannons and trained musketeers. Two years ago, their governor told this king that the Company could crush him.
S05|Now the Dutch are the ones who are trapped. Behind them, the monsoon sea is too rough for ships to come close. Inside the wall, the rice is running low. And the king outside is in no hurry.
S06|Soon, a single cannon shot will end this siege. And the Dutch commander will spend the rest of his life working for the king he came to beat. So, how did a trading company end up trapped on a beach, a short march from a king's palace? It starts with pepper.
S07|Kerala, the early seventeen hundreds. Picture a map, with north at the top.
S08|A long, thin strip of land runs down the west coast of India. On one side is the Arabian Sea. On the other is a wall of green hills called the Western Ghats. Between them, the land is wet, thick with coconut palms, and cut by rivers.
S09|On those hills grows black pepper, a small dried berry that Europeans pay high prices for. Ships have come for it for centuries.
S10|The coast is split into many small kingdoms. Kochi, Kollam, Kayamkulam, and a crowd of tiny ones, each with its own raja, a local king.
S11|The Dutch hold the fort at Kochi. They took it from the Portuguese in sixteen sixty-three. On our map, Dutch forts are orange.
S12|Their method is simple. They sign contracts with the small rajas. Sell your pepper only to us, at our price. And because each raja is small, none of them is strong enough to refuse. Remember that. The whole Dutch plan depends on the kings of this coast staying small.
S13|Now look south, to the very tip of the strip. Meet Marthanda Varma.
S14|He is born in seventeen oh-five. In seventeen twenty-nine, at about twenty-four, he becomes the ruler of Travancore. His capital is Padmanabhapuram, a palace town built of carved wood, about thirteen kilometres from the sea.
S15|Travancore is small. And his own nobles are not loyal. He spends his first years fighting powerful families at home who do not want him on the throne. He wins.
S16|Then he turns north. In the seventeen thirties, his army takes one neighbour after another. Attingal. The hill country of Nedumangad. Kottarakkara.
S17|And here is the problem for the Dutch. Each of those little kingdoms had signed a pepper contract with them. Once the land belongs to Travancore, the contract is gone. The pepper goes to whoever pays the most, and often that means the English.
S18|The Dutch needed the kings to stay small. This one is getting bigger every year.
S19|So, in seventeen thirty-nine, the Dutch send an important man. Meet Gustaaf Willem van Imhoff, the governor of Ceylon, the island known today as Sri Lanka.
S20|Van Imhoff comes to Kochi to fix the pepper problem. He writes that Dutch business on this coast is in complete ruin. And he believes the answer is force.
S21|He demands that Marthanda Varma give back one of the kingdoms he has taken, and return it to its exiled princess. If the king refuses, the Dutch will invade Travancore.
S22|The king's answer has become a famous story in Kerala. The historian M. O. Koshy recorded it like this. The king says that if the Dutch come, he will go into the forests. Van Imhoff says the Dutch will follow him there. And the king replies that he has been thinking about invading Europe one day.
S23|The story has been told many ways, and it may have grown in the telling. But the meaning is clear. The king is not afraid.
S24|Van Imhoff leaves. In November seventeen thirty-nine, the Dutch in Kerala declare war. They do it without permission from their own bosses in Batavia, the Company's capital in Asia, which is Jakarta today.
S25|At first, the war goes the Dutch way. The Dutch and their allies, the rajas of Kollam, Kayamkulam and Kochi, march on the Travancore army near Kollam.
S26|An exploding shell lands on the Travancore gunpowder store. It blows up. The Travancore soldiers run, and the Dutch capture sixteen cannons. Remember that gunpowder store.
S27|The allies push south toward Attingal and Varkala. But at Varkala they meet about five thousand Nairs, the warrior families of Kerala, men trained to fight with sword and shield from boyhood. The allies cannot break through, and they pull back.
S28|So, for most of seventeen forty, the war in the north goes back and forth. Neither side can finish it.
S29|Then, in late seventeen forty, the Dutch get about one hundred and seventy-five fresh soldiers from Ceylon. And they make a new plan. Stop fighting the king's army in the north. Strike him at home, in the south.
S30|On the twenty-sixth of November, seventeen forty, Dutch warships appear off the coast near Colachel. They fire on the shore. Three days later, the Dutch announce a blockade. No ship may trade with Travancore's coast.
