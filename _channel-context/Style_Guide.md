# Script Style Guide (summary)

This is a summary of the Style_Guide.docx in the Battlefield project, which was built from the Panipat (1526) and Plassey (1757) scripts. When in doubt, the full doc in the project is the authority.

## Voice
- Short sentences, mostly 4-12 words. Pace comes from stacking them. Longer sentences are simple actions joined by commas, never stacked clauses.
- Present tense for the story. "Will" flashes forward ("By midday, the Sultan of Delhi will be dead").
- Plain everyday words: big, huge, small, fast, strange. No academic words (hegemony, geopolitical, decisive engagement).
- No contractions at all: "does not", "cannot", "it is".
- The narrator never says "I" and gives no opinions. First person belongs only to historical figures, quoted.
- The viewer is addressed with commands: "Think about...", "Picture a map, with north at the top.", "Now look at the ground."
- "You" only for callbacks ("You know this one."). "Our" only for the on-screen map ("On our map, their blocks are gold.").
- Every foreign term, title or weapon gets a plain gloss in the same sentence.
- Comparisons stay inside the period (shields, gatekeepers, crowds at a gate). Nothing from modern sport, business or tech.
- Recurring starters: So, Now, Then, And now, But. "Remember". "About" or "perhaps" before numbers. "Later wrote" for primary sources.
- No hype words (epic, insane, legendary, shocking). Drama comes from the numbers and events.

## Structure
- Cold open on the battle morning in place-and-date fragments: "A village called Plassey, in Bengal. Dawn, the twenty-third of June, seventeen fifty-seven."
- Hook: the two sides with their numbers, what "should" happen, then "But" and the hidden edge.
- Bridge into backstory: "So, how did...?" answered by "It starts with...".
- New chapters open on a place-and-year fragment or a time marker. Turning points open with "Now" / "And now".
- Introduce key people with "Meet ...".
- Plant details with "Remember ..." and pay them off later ("You know this one.").
- Narrate the battle against the clock with time markers.
- Quote primary sources and name who wrote them.
- Flag every disputed detail openly ("Legend says", "One writer says", "Whatever the truth").
- Give one private moment of decision before the battle its own beat.
- Explain the outcome as several named causes working together.
- Close with a "So, why/was...?" question, a direct answer, then a compressed echo line ("Dawn. A mango grove. Three thousand men. One short fight, and a very long shadow.").
- Never "In this video" or "Welcome back". No like or subscribe asks mid-story.

## Script framework (from the training doc)
- Hook, then segments (each one Setup, Tension, Payoff), an optional mid-CTA at 20-50% (placed after a setup, used once), and an end-screen CTA.
- End-screen CTA in 3 steps: link back to this video, open a curiosity gap, promise the specific payoff of the next video.
- Put stakes in every setup, not only the hook.
